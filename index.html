About Me

"I, a universe of atoms, an atom in the universe." - Richard Feyman

I am a student driven by an almost unreasonable curiosity about how reality works.

My interests lie primarily in physics, mathematics, astronomy, and the philosophical questions that emerge when we push these fields to their limits. I am fascinated by the structure of the universe—from spacetime and quantum mechanics to black holes, cosmology, and the mathematical language that binds them together. I don’t just enjoy learning established theories; I’m drawn to the edges of knowledge, where explanations become incomplete and assumptions must be questioned.

I study with the long-term goal of pursuing advanced work in physics or aerospace engineering, and I approach learning as a craft rather than a checklist. I value depth over speed, understanding over memorization, and clarity over complexity for its own sake. When I encounter an idea, I don’t ask only how it works, but why it must work this way at all.

Writing is one of my primary tools for thinking. I use it to untangle difficult concepts, test arguments, and refine intuition. Some of what I write is explanatory—breaking down complex scientific ideas into accessible narratives. Some of it is speculative, where science, philosophy, and imagination meet. And some of it is reflective, documenting the process of learning itself: the confusion, the false starts, and the occasional moment of clarity.

Beyond individual study, I’m interested in building intellectual communities and meaningful projects. I enjoy creating initiatives that encourage curiosity, discussion, and collaboration—spaces where ideas are taken seriously and questions are valued more than quick answers. I believe that knowledge grows best when it is shared, challenged, and refined collectively.

I also care deeply about using science and technology for real-world impact. I’m drawn to ideas that combine technical rigor with social purpose—especially in education, accessibility, and long-term human progress. To me, intelligence is incomplete if it isn’t paired with responsibility.

Outside academics, I read widely, especially fiction that explores big ideas, identity, and the future of civilization. I play the piano, which reminds me that structure and creativity are not opposites—they’re partners. I find inspiration in stories of exploration, both scientific and human, and in thinkers who were willing to stand slightly apart from their time.

I don’t claim to have better answers.

I hope to ask better questions.
