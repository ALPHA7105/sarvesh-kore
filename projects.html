About Me

“I, a universe of atoms, an atom in the universe.” — Richard Feynman

I’m a student driven by curiosity — the kind that doesn’t switch off easily.

I’m most interested in physics, mathematics, and astronomy, especially where explanations start to feel incomplete. I’m fascinated by how the universe is structured, from spacetime and quantum mechanics to black holes and cosmology, and by the mathematics we use to make sense of it.

I study with the long-term goal of pursuing physics or aerospace engineering, but I care more about understanding than speed. When I learn something new, I’m usually less interested in memorizing results and more interested in why those results must be true at all.

Writing is one of the ways I think. I use it to work through ideas, test arguments, and make sense of things that don’t quite click yet — whether that means explaining a concept, exploring a speculative idea, or simply documenting the process of learning itself.

I’m also interested in building intellectual communities and meaningful projects — spaces where curiosity is encouraged, discussion is taken seriously, and good questions matter more than quick answers.

Outside academics, I read widely and play the piano, which constantly reminds me that structure and creativity aren’t opposites. I’m inspired by exploration, both scientific and human, and by people willing to think slightly ahead of their time.

I don’t claim to have better answers.

I’m trying to ask better questions.
